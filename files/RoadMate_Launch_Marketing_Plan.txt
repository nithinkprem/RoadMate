1. Go-to-Market Philosophy
RoadMate's value only exists once there is enough supply density in a small area for search results to feel reliable. The go-to-market plan is therefore supply-first and hyperlocal: win one set of Calicut neighbourhoods completely before expanding, rather than spreading thin across the city on day one.
1.1 Launch sequencing principle
Supply (verified shops/mechanics) is recruited and seeded before any consumer-facing marketing spend, so that the first wave of consumer users who try the product get a genuinely useful result rather than a sparse, disappointing directory.
2. Pre-Launch (Weeks -6 to 0)
2.1 Supply seeding
Use Google Places data to build an initial long-list of tyre shops, mechanics, battery dealers, and fuel-delivery contacts in the launch neighbourhoods (Section 3.4 of the PRD flags this as needing primary verification, not just scraping).
Visit or call the highest-density category (tyre/puncture shops) first, since that is the single most common breakdown and the best proof-of-concept category.
Offer free listing enrichment in exchange for accurate hours, pricing bands, and a photo — this is a manual, high-touch process for the first 100–150 listings, not a self-serve flow yet.
Target a minimum viable density before any public launch: at least ~15 verified listings per core category within the launch radius (see PRD Section 15.1 success metric).
2.2 Landing page & waitlist
A simple landing page collects waitlist signups and doubles as an early willingness-to-pay signal for the future Premium Membership (Section 4 open research item in the PRD).
Local SEO groundwork: Google Business Profile presence, city + category landing pages ("tyre puncture shop near Calicut") built for organic search, launched ahead of the product itself.

3. Launch Week
3.1 Channels
Channel
Role at launch
Local WhatsApp & community groups
Highest-trust channel in Kerala for a hyperlocal service; direct outreach in resident and commuter groups covering the launch neighbourhoods
Instagram/Facebook local ads
Geo-targeted ads within the launch radius, creative built around the "breakdown at night" and "who do I even call" pain points
Shop-partner cross-promotion
Recruited shops display a RoadMate window sticker/QR code, converting their existing walk-in traffic into app awareness
Local press / city blogs
A Calicut-focused tech/startup story angle: "Google Maps + Swiggy, for the roadside"
College and ride-share/delivery-rider communities
High breakdown frequency, high smartphone fluency, strong word-of-mouth propagation
3.2 Launch-week goals
Hit the minimum viable listing density in at least 2–3 launch neighbourhoods before any paid promotion begins.
Track the Section 15.1 PRD metrics from day one: weekly active searchers, search→action rate, review submission rate.
Run a manual daily check on "open now" accuracy for the first two weeks — this is the single fastest way to lose first-time-user trust (see PRD Risk table, Section 19).
4. Post-Launch Growth (Months 1–6)
4.1 Neighbourhood-by-neighbourhood expansion
Rather than opening all of Calicut at once, expand ward-by-ward as each new area reaches the minimum listing density, using the same manual-recruitment playbook proven in launch week. This keeps the "is this actually useful near me" experience consistently strong as the footprint grows.
4.2 Retention loop
Push/SMS re-engagement is deliberately deferred until Phase 2 notifications infrastructure exists; in Phase 1, retention is driven by search quality and word-of-mouth alone.
Shop-side retention: a simple weekly WhatsApp check-in to keep hours/status fresh doubles as a light-touch relationship-management channel until self-serve owner accounts exist.
4.3 SEO plan
Public, indexable shop-detail pages (Section 2.2's Next.js SSR choice supports this) become long-tail SEO assets — "[category] near [neighbourhood], Calicut" pages.
Structured data (schema.org LocalBusiness) on shop pages to support rich search results.
Content: a small set of practical, non-salesy guides ("what to do if your bike won't start at night") that naturally link into the search flow.

6. Expansion Roadmap
Stage
Trigger to proceed
Calicut (launch city)
Baseline — minimum listing density and Phase 1 success metrics achieved (PRD Section 15.1)
Kochi
Calicut shows sustained repeat usage and a validated recruitment playbook that a small team can replicate without founder-level hand-holding
Trivandrum
Kochi launch replicates Calicut's metrics within a comparable timeframe, confirming the playbook (not just the city) generalizes
Kerala-wide
At least 2 successful city launches with consistent unit economics on commission/listing revenue
India
Kerala-wide operations are profitable or on a clear path, and the worker-verification and payments infrastructure (Phase 2–3) has proven reliable at multi-city scale
Each expansion stage reuses the same phased product roadmap (PRD Section 7) rather than requiring a new build — the product's phase gates are about capability maturity, not city count, so a new city launch is a go-to-market exercise, not a re-engineering one.
